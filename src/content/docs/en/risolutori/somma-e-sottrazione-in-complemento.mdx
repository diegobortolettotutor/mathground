Given two integers $A$ and $B$ and a number of bits $n$ ($4$, $8$, $12$ or $16$), this
solver computes $A + B$ or $A - B$ **twice**, in two's complement and in ones'
complement, so that the two procedures can be compared. The numbers can be entered in
decimal or directly in binary.

For the meaning of ones' and two's complement and for computing the opposite of a
number, see the page
[Ones' complement and two's complement](/en/risolutori/complemento-a-1-e-a-2/).

## Addition

In both systems the addition is done **column by column**, as for natural numbers, from
right to left and with carries. The sign bit is added like all the others: it needs no
special treatment. The difference between the two systems lies in the carry leaving the
leftmost bit.

- **Two's complement**: the outgoing carry is **discarded**.
- **Ones' complement**: the outgoing carry is **added back** (end-around carry), that is
  $1$ is added to the result starting from the rightmost column. This second addition
  never produces a new outgoing carry.

## Subtraction

Subtraction becomes an addition with the opposite: $A - B = A + (-B)$. The opposite of
$B$ is obtained by inverting the bits and adding $1$ in two's complement, by inverting
the bits only in ones' complement. In this way the same circuit performs both additions
and subtractions.

## Overflow

With $n$ bits a limited range of numbers can be represented: if the true result falls
outside the range there is **overflow**. The computation is carried out anyway, but the
bits obtained, read as signed, give a wrong number, of opposite sign to the true one.

Overflow **is not the same as the outgoing carry**: in two's complement the outgoing
carry is discarded with no consequences, and overflow can occur even without any
outgoing carry. Two rules are used to detect it.

- **Sign rule** (valid in both systems). If the two addends have different signs,
  overflow is impossible, because the sum lies between them. If they have the same sign,
  there is overflow when the result has the opposite sign.
- **Carry rule** (two's complement). There is overflow when the carry entering the
  sign-bit column differs from the carry leaving it.

Subtraction in two's complement has a special case: if $B = -2^{n-1}$, the most negative
number, its opposite is not representable and inverting the bits and adding $1$ gives
back the same bits as $B$. The two rules, applied to $A + (-B)$, are no longer reliable
and overflow is decided from the true value: it occurs if and only if $A \geq 0$.

| Number of bits | Ones' complement | Two's complement |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $4$ | from $-7$ to $7$ | from $-8$ to $7$ |
| $8$ | from $-127$ to $127$ | from $-128$ to $127$ |
| $12$ | from $-2047$ to $2047$ | from $-2048$ to $2047$ |
| $16$ | from $-32767$ to $32767$ | from $-32768$ to $32767$ |

## Solver

Choose the number of bits and the operation, then the input mode.

- **In decimal**: write the two integers, optionally preceded by a $+$ or $-$ sign. The
  solver writes them in binary, represents them in the two systems and carries out the
  computation. The number $-2^{n-1}$ is accepted, but it exists only in two's
  complement: in that case ones' complement is skipped.
- **In binary**: enter the bits in the boxes. The same bits are read differently in the
  two systems when the sign bit is $1$: in ones' complement they are worth one unit more
  than in two's complement.
