This is the variant of the Gram-Schmidt process that stops at
**orthogonalization**: starting from three vectors $\vec v_1,\vec v_2,\vec v_3$ in
$\mathbb{R}^3$, it produces three mutually orthogonal vectors $\vec w_1,\vec w_2,\vec w_3$
that are **not** of length $1$ (the final normalization step is missing). The tips of the
vectors can be dragged in "Edit v₁ v₂ v₃" mode.

## The process, step by step

<span style="display:inline-block;width:0.6em;height:0.6em;border-radius:50%;background:#3b82f6;margin-right:0.4em;vertical-align:middle;"></span>**Step 1.**
The first resulting vector coincides with $\vec v_1$: nothing needs to be subtracted or
normalized.

$$\vec w_1=\vec v_1$$

<span style="display:inline-block;width:0.6em;height:0.6em;border-radius:50%;background:#10b981;margin-right:0.4em;vertical-align:middle;"></span>**Step 2.**
The projection of $\vec v_2$ onto $\vec w_1$ is subtracted. Since $\vec w_1$ is not a unit
vector, the projection coefficient is $\dfrac{\vec v_2\cdot\vec w_1}{\vec w_1\cdot\vec w_1}$
(the dot product alone is not enough, as it would be with a unit vector):

$$\vec w_2=\vec v_2-\dfrac{\vec v_2\cdot\vec w_1}{\vec w_1\cdot\vec w_1}\,\vec w_1$$

<span style="display:inline-block;width:0.6em;height:0.6em;border-radius:50%;background:#f59e0b;margin-right:0.4em;vertical-align:middle;"></span>**Step 3.**
**Both** projections are subtracted from $\vec v_3$, onto $\vec w_1$ and onto $\vec w_2$,
using the same kind of coefficient normalized by the squared norm of the vector being
projected onto:

$$\vec w_3=\vec v_3-\dfrac{\vec v_3\cdot\vec w_1}{\vec w_1\cdot\vec w_1}\,\vec w_1-\dfrac{\vec v_3\cdot\vec w_2}{\vec w_2\cdot\vec w_2}\,\vec w_2$$

Pressing "Compute & keep" animates this sequence on the simulator, color by color, in the
same order described above. The "slow motion" slider allows following it at a gentler
pace, without changing the final result.

## Checking orthogonality

Right below the simulator's buttons, two numeric rows update live while the starting
vectors are dragged:

- the **norms** $\lVert\vec w_1\rVert,\lVert\vec w_2\rVert,\lVert\vec w_3\rVert$, marked
  with "$\neq 1$" because, unlike the orthonormalized version, they are **not** expected
  to equal $1$: the length of the resulting vectors depends on the starting vectors;
- the **cross dot products** $\vec w_1\cdot\vec w_2,\ \vec w_1\cdot\vec w_3,\
  \vec w_2\cdot\vec w_3$, which should instead stay at $0$ (the resulting vectors are
  pairwise orthogonal, even though not unit length).

A zero dot product is, on its own, the defining property of orthogonality: that live
number offers a way to check, for any linearly independent starting triple, that the
process worked correctly, regardless of the length of the resulting vectors.
